# C&I Solar PPA, Solar + Battery and BESS Adoption in Thailand by Customer Industry

Research date: 2026-11-09. Scope: Thailand only. Every number carries its year. Labels used: [official] = regulator/government or sourced via one; [company] = company disclosure; [press] = news report; [vendor/marketing] = installer or vendor material; [estimate] = third-party market research or modelled estimate.

**Headline caveat:** I found **no public source that gives the share of Thai C&I rooftop solar or private PPA capacity by industry** (no % by F&B, automotive, electronics, etc.). DEDE, ERC, Krungsri Research and IEEFA all report capacity by segment (utility, rooftop, C&I vs residential) but not by industry. Any industry ranking below is **inferred from named deals and developer targeting**, not measured shares.

## 1. Market size of Thai C&I rooftop solar / private PPA, and which industries dominate

### Takeaway
Thailand had roughly 3.3–3.6 GW of rooftop solar by end-2024/2025, of which about 2.2 GW (about 60%) is C&I (as of Jan 2026). Most of it is self-consumption, owned outright or financed by a third party under a private PPA. The private PPA market is still small in value: one company-sourced estimate puts it at about THB 2 billion in 2024. No measured industry split exists. Named deals point to manufacturing as the core buyer group: electronics/HDD, auto parts, F&B, rubber/plastics/packaging, chemicals, steel/metals and cement. Malls, hotels, hospitals, universities and data centers are secondary or emerging.

### Cited Findings
**Total rooftop and C&I capacity**
- Rooftop solar capacity by year: 1,829 MW (2022), 2,378 MW (2023), 3,310 MW (2024), 3,600 MW (2025). - "The C&I sector dominated rooftop installations, accounting for 2.2GW of the total 3.6GW as of January 2026." IEEFA cites TransitionZero (20 Jan 2026) for this — [IEEFA, Aug 2026](https://ieefa.org/sites/default/files/2026-08/IEEFA%20Report_Reforming%20Thailand's%20rooftop%20solar%20policy%20framework%20to%20reduce%20gas%20dependence_August%202026.pdf)
- TransitionZero's satellite/ML estimate is about 3.60 GWac of rooftop solar nationwide (Jan 2026), with a range of about 3.02–4.17 GW, noted as higher than official figures. ### Inferences
- Rough C&I rooftop scale: about 2.2 GW cumulative (Jan 2026). Annual additions across all rooftop were about 550 MW (2022→2023), about 930 MW (2023→2024) and about 290 MW (2024→2025), from IEEFA's year-end series. C&I is likely the majority of each year's additions. This is my arithmetic; the series mixes official and estimated bases.
- **Qualitative industry ranking (by visible MW in deal flow, not market share):** (1) electronics/HDD and electrical appliances (largest single deals: Seagate about 21 MW, Midea 12 MW, Cal-Comp 10 MW); (2) metals/steel and cement/building materials (Tata Steel 11.8 MW; SCG Cement 50 MW MOU); (3) automotive and auto parts (many 1–4 MW sites in Rayong/Chonburi/Chachoengsao/EEC); (4) food & beverage / agri-food (many 0.5–3 MW sites, e.g. Thai President Foods, GFPT); (5) rubber, plastics, packaging and textiles (many sub-2.5 MW sites); then (6) chemicals/glass; (7) hotels, malls, hospitals and universities (fewer, smaller); (8) data centers (emerging, mostly via utility-scale/direct PPA rather than rooftop). This ranking reflects which deals get press and EPC listings, and is biased toward large and listed firms.
- Industries with high, flat daytime load, large roofs and export/multinational parents (Japanese, US, EU) dominate. This matches the developers' stated targeting of industrial estates and factories.

- Annual C&I-only MW additions for 2022–2025 were not found separately from residential.
- Other developers' rooftop portfolios: a search summary mentioned GUNKUL about 94.6 MW across 108 rooftop projects and Banpu NEXT 67.0 MW of Thai rooftop. I could not verify the underlying page or year, so treat these as unverified.

## 3. BESS for C&I in Thailand: adopters, market size, projects, economics

### Takeaway
C&I BESS in Thailand is early-stage. Projects are small (hundreds of kWh to a few MWh) and justified mainly by **backup/power quality and solar shifting**, not pure peak shaving. Thailand's narrow peak/off-peak spread and demand-charge structure give weak arbitrage economics. Adopters seen so far are manufacturers with sensitive processes (batteries, textiles, rubber, electronics) and large solar hosts. Market size figures are third-party estimates that conflict.

### Cited Findings
- A Symbior Solar executive said the gap between peak and off-peak prices in Thailand is small, limiting peak-shaving revenue. He sees C&I demand for storage **for backup** as the "low-hanging fruit" [press] — [Energy-Storage.news, "Thailand's energy storage market lags…"](https://www.energy-storage.news/thailands-energy-storage-market-lags-despite-renewable-push-and-upstream-manufacturing-support/) (page returned 403; quote via search snippet)
- **Tokyo Gas Engineering Solutions** (METI Global South project): about 20 MW PV plus about 20 MWh BESS across multiple high-demand Thai factory sites under an **on-site PPA**. Surplus midday PV charges batteries, which discharge in evening/peak hours. Valued at about JPY 3.7 bn (about USD 22.8 m). Factories and industries are not named (Jul 2026) — [ESS News, 13 Jul 2026](https://www.ess-news.com/2026/07/13/tokyo-gas-unit-to-test-bess-pv-model-in-thailand-factories/)
- C&I BESS installs in EPC references: **GS Yuasa Siam Industry** (battery maker, Chachoengsao) 522 kWh (May 2026); **Kangwal Spinning** (textiles, Samut Sakhon) 2,507 kWh (May 2026); **Pongpara Rubber** 215 kWh alongside a 2.26 MWp PV (Nov 2025) [vendor/EPC reference] — [Neoclean](https://www.neoclean.co.th/categorycontent/975/project-references)
- SINEXCEL commissioned its first Thai C&I storage project (5 × Sirius 135K units) for **backup power** for local C&I operations [vendor PR] — [Energy-Storage.news industry update](https://www.energy-storage.news/industry-updates/sinexcel-accelerates-apac-expansion-with-sirius-135k-energy-storage-project-in-thailand)

- Hitachi ABB Power Grids battery storage for a 214 MW industrial microgrid at Saha Group's industrial park, Sriracha (date and status not verified) — [Energy-Storage.news](https://www.energy-storage.news/news/hitachi-abb-power-grids-battery-storage-to-be-used-at-214mw-industrial-micr)

- Utility-side context: the 2022–2030 FiT includes **1,000 MW of ground-mounted solar + BESS** at THB 2.83/kWh (vs THB 2.17 for solar only), 25-year contracts. ### Inferences
- Early C&I BESS adopters are factories where outages or voltage dips are costly (continuous-process textiles, battery manufacturing, rubber, electronics) and sites with large rooftop PV that wants to raise self-consumption, rather than pure peak-shaving buyers.
- Implied savings from the vendor case: THB 280k/yr on 500 kWh is about THB 560/kWh-yr. At a THB 4,500–10,000/kWh capex range, simple payback would be about 8–18 years. This is my arithmetic, consistent with the "borderline" characterisation. No independent IRR or payback figure for Thai C&I BESS was found.
- The shift of the system peak to the evening, plus a possible future TOU redesign, is the main upside for C&I solar + storage PPAs. The Tokyo Gas pilot is testing exactly this.

### Gaps
- No credible MW/MWh figure for installed C&I BESS in Thailand, and no industry split.
- No published PEA/MEA demand-charge-based payback study for Thai C&I BESS was found.

## 4. Typical C&I solar PPA terms and why some industries adopt more

### Takeaway
Thai C&I private PPAs are zero-capex, developer-owned rooftop systems. The customer pays per kWh at a **discount of about 10–30% to the PEA/MEA tariff** (one outlier claim of 38.5%), over **15–20 years** (some 20–25). Typical factory system sizes are **about 0.5–3 MWp**, with large anchors at 10–21 MW. Adoption is driven by daytime load, retail tariffs above THB 4/kWh, deregulated permitting (2024–25), and Scope 2 / RE100 pressure on export-oriented multinationals.

### Cited Findings
- Private PPAs fall under general civil law (freedom of contract). Common rooftop structure: the factory leases its roof to the developer, who owns, operates and maintains the system and sells power under a corporate PPA [legal] — [Norton Rose Fulbright, Asia renewables snapshot: Thailand](https://www.nortonrosefulbright.com/en/knowledge/publications/2f5545da/asia-renewables-snapshot-thailand)
- Discount: "ต่ำกว่าค่าไฟฟ้าปกติประมาณ 10-30% เป็นระยะเวลายาวนานตามสัญญา 15 ถึง 20 ปี" (about 10–30% below the normal tariff, for 15–20 years). ESCOs carry 100% of the installation cost [Thai press] — [Thansettakij](https://www.thansettakij.com/economy/energy/661797); ALT offered a 15-year PPA with ">15%" savings [press] — [InfoQuest](https://www.infoquest.co.th/?p=169210)
- Walailak University rooftop: claimed 38.5% discount vs PEA rate (2024) [vendor claim] — [SolarQuarter](https://solarquarter.com/2024/03/01/prime-road-powers-joint-venture-secures-rooftop-solar-project-at-walailak-university-in-thailand/)
- Marubeni Thai unit: 20-year PPA for 768 kWp rooftop (Jun 2025) [press, via search summary] — [pv magazine (context)](https://www.pv-magazine.com/2026/09/10/thailand-paves-the-way-for-10-gw-of-rooftop-solar/)
- Retail tariff context: average retail about THB 3.88/kWh — [IEEFA, Aug 2026](https://ieefa.org/sites/default/files/2026-08/IEEFA%20Report_Reforming%20Thailand's%20rooftop%20solar%20policy%20framework%20to%20reduce%20gas%20dependence_August%202026.pdf); Krungsri expects prices to stay above THB 4.00/unit (2025) — [Krungsri Research](https://www.krungsri.com/en/research/research-intelligence/solar-rooftop-2025)
- Typical sizes: WHAUP average about 1.4 MW per contract (2024); EPC references cluster at 0.4–3 MWp, with many just under 1,000 kWp (e.g. 997.92, 999.75, 999.44 kWp). Large anchors: Seagate 20.96 MW, Midea 12 MWp, Cal-Comp 10 MWp — [Kaohoon](https://www.kaohooninternational.com/?p=578004); [Neoclean](https://www.neoclean.co.th/categorycontent/975/project-references)
- Regulatory easing that favours industry: a 2024 Ministerial Regulation removed the factory licence (Ror.Ngor.4) requirement for rooftop solar outside industrial estates regardless of size (previously required above 1,000 kW). Rooftops up to 20 kg/m² are exempt from building-modification permits. ERC exempts systems ≤10 kVA from licensing, and PEA/MEA connection moved online — [IEEFA Appendix I, Aug 2026](https://ieefa.org/sites/default/files/2026-08/IEEFA%20Report_Reforming%20Thailand's%20rooftop%20solar%20policy%20framework%20to%20reduce%20gas%20dependence_August%202026.pdf)
- IEEFA says C&I dominance is "driven by the availability of a long-term FiT for larger C&I systems and by growing corporate demand for renewable energy to support decarbonization goals." The corporate tax deduction of up to 150% for certified energy-saving equipment (including solar) cannot be combined with BOI/EEC incentives — [IEEFA, Aug 2026](https://ieefa.org/sites/default/files/2026-08/IEEFA%20Report_Reforming%20Thailand's%20rooftop%20solar%20policy%20framework%20to%20reduce%20gas%20dependence_August%202026.pdf)
- Krungsri: private organisations turned to rooftop solar to cover **daytime** electricity needs. Rooftop under FiT in central Thailand has a 6.1-year payback and 15% IRR (2025) — [Krungsri Research](https://www.krungsri.com/en/research/research-intelligence/solar-rooftop-2025)
- Direct PPA / third-party access pilot (Oct 2025 "Quick Big Win") is limited to **BOI-promoted data centers**; still in pilot as of May 2026 — [IEEFA](https://ieefa.org/sites/default/files/2026-08/IEEFA%20Report_Reforming%20Thailand's%20rooftop%20solar%20policy%20framework%20to%20reduce%20gas%20dependence_August%202026.pdf); [CapSolar](https://capsolar.co.th/en/knowledge/solar-law-thailand-2569)
- The draft PDP 2026 targets up to 10 GW of rooftop procurement (draft, Sep 2026) — [pv magazine, 10 Sep 2026](https://www.pv-magazine.com/2026/09/10/thailand-paves-the-way-for-10-gw-of-rooftop-solar/)

### Inferences
- Why manufacturing leads: (a) daytime 1–3 shift loads match the solar profile and reach near-100% self-consumption, with no export needed given the no-export/low-buyback regime; (b) large flat roofs in industrial estates; (c) Japanese, US and EU parents with RE100 / SBTi / Scope 2 targets (e.g. Seagate, Midea, Mitsubishi Electric, Toyota supply chain, BASF); (d) exporters to the EU face CBAM (steel, aluminium, cement, fertiliser) and customer carbon-disclosure demands. Note that CBAM covers Scope 1/process emissions, plus indirect electricity emissions only in some sectors; I found no Thai source directly linking CBAM to rooftop PPA uptake.
- Why commercial lags: malls, hotels and hospitals have evening/24h load and less roof area per kWh consumed. Data centers have 24/7 load far exceeding roof potential and so are pushed toward off-site/direct PPA rather than rooftop.
- The loss of BOI incentives for self-use solar from 1 Jul 2025 likely tilts owner-financed projects toward third-party PPAs, which need no capex (inference).

### Gaps
- No standardised, sourced dataset of PPA tariff discounts by developer or year; the 10–30% range comes from press and vendor sources.
- Escalation clauses, buy-out options and take-or-pay terms for Thai PPAs were not found in primary sources.

## 5. Surveys or reports segmenting Thai C&I customers by size and industry interest in solar/BESS

### Takeaway
I found **no public survey** that segments Thai C&I customers by size (SME / medium / large) and industry interest in solar or BESS. The available segmentation is tariff-based (PEA/MEA small, medium and large general service) and policy-based (FiT size bands).

### Cited Findings
- Historic FiT banding by factory size: THB 6.40/kWh for small factories and THB 6.01/kWh for medium and large factories (250–1,000 kWp) under the 2013–15 rooftop FiT. A 2018 pilot later gave 100 MW to commercial rooftop systems (10–1,000 kW) — [IEEFA Appendix I](https://ieefa.org/sites/default/files/2026-08/IEEFA%20Report_Reforming%20Thailand's%20rooftop%20solar%20policy%20framework%20to%20reduce%20gas%20dependence_August%202026.pdf)
- The 2021 net-billing extension carved out 50 MWp for public-benefit users: 20 MWp hospitals, 20 MWp academic institutions, 10 MWp agricultural water pumping — [IEEFA Appendix I](https://ieefa.org/sites/default/files/2026-08/IEEFA%20Report_Reforming%20Thailand's%20rooftop%20solar%20policy%20framework%20to%20reduce%20gas%20dependence_August%202026.pdf)
- Frost & Sullivan published a "Thailand Residential and B2B Solar Rooftop Installations Forecast to 2026" (2021) that may segment B2B. It is paywalled and was not reviewed [estimate] — [Frost store](https://store.frost.com/thailand-residential-and-b2b-solar-rooftop-installations-forecast-to-2026.html); [BusinessWire summary](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20210915005769/en/Thailand-Residential-and-B2B-Solar-Rooftop-Installations-Markets-2021-2026-Innovation-Culture-in-Business-Models-and-Strategic-Partnerships-to-Push-Solar-Projects-Amidst-Challenging-Times---ResearchAndMarkets.com)
- Krungthai COMPASS published a 2021 note on Solar Corporate PPAs (dated) — [Thaipublica, Aug 2021](https://thaipublica.org/2021/08/krungthai-compass31/)

### Inferences
- In practice, the private PPA market serves medium and large general-service customers (PEA/MEA large-business TOU tariffs, typically more than 0.3–1 MW of demand). SMEs are served mainly by EPC/ownership sales or small leases. This follows from the typical 0.5–3 MWp contract size and is an inference.

### Gaps
- No Thai survey (e.g. FTI / Federation of Thai Industries, Kasikorn Research, SCB EIC, BOI) with industry-level adoption or interest rates for solar/BESS was found within the tool budget. Recommended next checks: SCB EIC and Kasikorn Research notes on solar rooftop/ESG for SMEs; FTI Renewable Energy Industry Club; ERC's annual report on self-generation registrations; PEA's annual statistics of customers with solar by tariff class.
